## Blog 1
Why does learning data science in social and interdisciplinary contexts matter?

We model social topics and people because data can help us understand patterns in how people behave, interact, and respond to different situations. Looking at social data can help us learn more about communities, trends, and problems that affect different groups of people. For example, data can be used to study things like education, income, social media use, crime, health, or public opinion. Modeling these topics can help researchers and organizations make better decisions because they have evidence to support their ideas instead of just guessing. At the same time, it is important to remember that people are more complicated than numbers, so models should be used as tools to help us understand society rather than explain everything about a person.
When using data about people, there are several important responsibilities. One of the biggest is protecting people's privacy and making sure personal information is not shared or used in harmful ways. We also have to make sure the data is accurate and collected fairly. If the data is biased or incomplete, the results could give the wrong impression about certain people or groups. Another responsibility is being careful about how conclusions are made from the data. We should not stereotype or unfairly judge people based only on numbers. Overall, using data about people should be done respectfully, honestly, and ethically.
Through this course, I am hoping to better understand how data can be used to study real-world social issues and human behavior. I want to learn more about how researchers collect, organize, and analyze social data to find meaningful patterns. I also want to understand how to tell whether data and conclusions are reliable. Another thing I hope to learn is how ethics plays a role in data science, especially when working with information about real people. By the end of the course, I hope to feel more confident using data while also understanding the responsibilities that come with it. Lastly, by the end of this course I hope to be able to read complex code quickly and understand what it is that the code does.
